Southern Utah's national parks draw travelers from everywhere — Arches and Canyonlands out of [Moab](/locations/utah/moab), Zion out of [Springdale](/locations/utah/springdale), and the fast-growing vacation market around [St. George](/locations/utah/st-george). Almost every trip out here shares the same shape: a group of family or friends, a vacation rental, and a few days of hiking, biking, or exploring that end with everyone back at the house, exhausted and hungry.

A private hibachi chef has become one of the most requested additions to exactly that kind of trip — because after a day on the trail, nobody wants to load a group of twelve into cars and find a restaurant that can seat everyone.

## Why Vacation Rental Dinners Are the Default in Moab and Springdale

Moab and Springdale are almost entirely vacation-rental economies — houses built for groups, with the kind of patio or backyard space that makes an outdoor group dinner easy to host. Our chefs arrive fully self-contained: a propane teppan grill that needs no gas hookup, all ingredients, and everything required to run the full show on your rental's patio or backyard. There's nothing to coordinate with the property beyond a flat outdoor space of at least 10×10 feet.

For a [Moab](/locations/utah/moab) group coming off a day on the Slickrock Trail or a hike through Arches, or a [Springdale](/locations/utah/springdale) group back from the Narrows or Angels Landing, that means the best meal of the trip happens without anyone having to leave the house.

## Family Reunions Built Around a National Park Trip

Multi-generational family reunions are the single most common occasion we see in Southern Utah — grandparents, parents, and grandkids all converging on a rental house near [St. George](/locations/utah/st-george) or Springdale for a long weekend anchored around a national park visit. Private hibachi gives a group like that one shared night where everyone is at the same table, watching the same show, without anyone needing to cook or coordinate a restaurant reservation for a party of twenty.

St. George in particular has become a strong reunion market thanks to the Entrada and Desert Color resort communities, which offer the kind of large, well-equipped rental properties that make hosting a big group dinner easy.

## What to Expect from a Mobile Hibachi Event

**Fully self-contained setup:** your chef brings a propane teppan grill, so there's no need for gas hookups or special permissions from the rental property — this is the single biggest logistics question we get from vacation rental guests, and the answer is simple: we bring everything.

**All proteins cooked to order:** the standard menu plus premium upgrades — filet mignon, lobster tail, wagyu — available on request.

**The full show:** fire tricks, flying shrimp, and precision knife work, performed live for your group over 90–120 minutes.

**Complete cleanup:** your chef packs out everything when dinner is done. The rental is left exactly as it was.

**Group size:** one chef station comfortably serves up to 20–25 guests. For larger family reunions, we bring a second station.

## When to Book

Southern Utah's peak season is spring and fall, when temperatures are ideal for hiking — book 2–3 weeks ahead for those windows. Summer dates, while hotter, can often be booked with less advance notice. If your trip includes a specific date you can't move, reserving early is always the safer call, especially for larger group bookings.

**Pricing:** starting at $60 per adult, $30 per child (ages 4–12), $600 event minimum.
