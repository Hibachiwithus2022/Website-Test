Booking a private hibachi chef at an Airbnb is one of the best upgrades you can make to a rental weekend — and it works at most properties. Here's how to make it happen without any host friction.

---

## Can You Actually Do This at an Airbnb?

Yes, at most properties. The chef arrives with a fully self-contained propane teppan grill — no gas hookup, no kitchen access required, no permanent installation. Setup happens on any flat outdoor surface: a deck, patio, backyard, or covered terrace.

The key question to ask your Airbnb host: **Is open-flame outdoor cooking permitted at the property?**

Most Airbnb listings with outdoor space allow it. Properties with covered decks or HOA restrictions sometimes have rules against open-flame grills. If the listing doesn't mention it, a quick message to the host before booking the chef is all it takes.

---

## How to Message Your Host

Keep it simple. Here's the message that works:

> "Hi — we're planning to have a private chef cook at the property. They use a self-contained propane teppan grill on the outdoor deck or patio area. There's no hookup required and the chef handles all cleanup. Is outdoor open-flame cooking permitted at your property?"

Most hosts say yes immediately. The ones who have restrictions will tell you, and you can ask whether there's a workaround — a different setup location, a ground-level area rather than a deck, etc.

**One rule:** Never book the chef before confirming with the host. Once you have confirmation, everything else is straightforward.

---

## What Setup Space You Need

Approximately 10×10 feet of flat outdoor clearance. That's it.

The most common Airbnb setups:

- **Deck or patio** — most common; the chef sets up at one end of the deck, guests sit around the grill
- **Backyard lawn** — works well for larger groups (20+ guests) who need more space
- **Covered terrace** — adds weather protection; confirm that the ceiling clearance is adequate for cooking (most covered terraces are fine)

What doesn't work: indoor setups (propane grills are outdoor-only), balconies with less than 10 feet of clearance, or properties with explicit no-open-flame rules.

## Booking the Chef: Timing

Do it early. The most common Airbnb hibachi request pattern is:

1. Guest books the rental → realizes a private chef would make the weekend → contacts us the week before arrival

That works in most markets for mid-week events. But for **weekend rentals in high-demand markets** — beach towns, ski destinations, major cities — book the chef when you book the property.

High-demand Airbnb markets where early booking matters:

- [Newport, RI](/locations/rhode-island/newport) — bachelorette and summer rental capital of southern New England; peak dates fill 3–5 weeks ahead
- [Aspen, CO](/locations/colorado/aspen) and [Vail, CO](/locations/colorado/vail) — ski season and summer festival weekends book 6–10 weeks ahead
- [Lake Geneva, WI](/locations/wisconsin/lake-geneva) — Shore Path bachelorette weekends and family reunion rentals book 4–6 weeks ahead
- [Gatlinburg, TN](/locations/tennessee/gatlinburg) and [Pigeon Forge, TN](/locations/tennessee/pigeon-forge) — cabin rental capital of the Smokies; peak Smoky Mountain weekends fill fast
- [Narragansett, RI](/locations/rhode-island/narragansett) and [Westerly, RI](/locations/rhode-island/westerly) — South County beach house rental market; July and August book 3–4 weeks ahead

Off-peak and mid-week events are typically available with 1–2 weeks' notice in most markets.

## What the Chef Brings

Everything.

- Propane teppan grill — fully self-contained, no hookup required
- All proteins: chicken, steak, shrimp, salmon, vegetables, fried rice, lo mein, miso soup, salad
- House-made ginger sauce, yum yum sauce, teriyaki
- All plates, chopsticks, and utensils
- Full cleanup when dinner ends — the chef leaves the area exactly as they found it

What you provide: seating for your guests and vehicle access to the property.

**Premium upgrades** (order at booking): filet mignon, lobster tail, Chilean sea bass, king shrimp, wagyu beef — per guest, mixed and matched however the group wants.

---

## The Cleanup Question (Hosts Always Ask)

The chef handles it. The grill, the prep materials, the cooking waste, the utensils — all of it leaves with the chef. The only cleanup on your end is the plates and cups your guests use, same as any other meal.

This is one of the reasons Airbnb hosts say yes more often than no. The event doesn't affect the property, the cleanup doesn't fall on the host, and the guests have a dramatically better experience.
