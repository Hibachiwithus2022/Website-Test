## Paradise Valley Estate Setups

[Paradise Valley](/locations/arizona/paradise-valley) homes are purpose-built for this kind of event. The common outdoor setup — a large covered patio or ramada adjacent to the pool, with a flat stone or pavers surface and adequate clearance — is essentially ideal for hibachi. The chef's teppan station fits cleanly, guests can be arranged in a natural arc, and the covered structure handles both the Arizona heat and the occasional cooler winter evening.

The key logistics for Paradise Valley estate events:

**Surface and space:** The chef needs a flat, non-combustible surface with at least a 12×12 foot footprint for the station and working area. Stone pavers, concrete decking, and tile surfaces all work. No grass directly under the grill.

**Covered outdoor structures:** Ramadas and covered patios work well. Full enclosures with no ventilation don't — teppanyaki cooking uses an open propane flame that requires open-air clearance above. Semi-open covered structures with open sides are fine.

**Guest count and station count:** Single station for 10–18 guests. For estate dinner parties of 20–35, two stations allow everyone a front-row experience simultaneously rather than in rotation.

**Season:** October through April is Arizona's prime outdoor entertaining window. The evenings are cool, the desert views are dramatic in evening light, and outdoor events run without the heat logistics of summer.

**Summer events:** May through September outdoor events work well with early evening starts (5:30–6:30 PM) before temperatures climb. Covered ramadas and misters handle most summer evenings in the 95°–100° range. Confirm timing when booking.
