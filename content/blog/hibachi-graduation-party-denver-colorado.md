Colorado's Front Range graduation party season is the most competitive private hibachi booking window in the state — and it runs almost entirely on two months: May and June.

The format that makes it work is simple: a private hibachi chef arrives at your backyard with a fully self-contained propane teppan grill, all proteins, and a live cooking performance that transforms any Denver metro residential lot into a genuine event. No restaurant reservation that seats only half the family. No driving after the ceremony. Your backyard, your timeline, your guest list — and you're actually at your own event instead of managing the kitchen.

This is the complete guide to booking private hibachi for a Front Range graduation party.

## When to Book

The graduation party booking calendar in Colorado runs on school district and university calendars.

**High school graduation parties (Douglas, Arapahoe, Jefferson counties):**
- Graduation ceremonies typically run the third and fourth weekends of May (some districts extend into early June)
- The Saturday after graduation ceremony is the most requested hibachi date
- Book **4–6 weeks ahead** for any May or June Saturday
- Mid-April is the practical deadline for May graduation Saturdays

**[Highlands Ranch](/locations/colorado/highlands-ranch):** Douglas County's most active graduation party market. Thunderridge, Rock Canyon, and Mountain Vista High School graduation ceremonies generate concentrated Saturday demand across a 3-week window. If your graduation date is in May, submit it in early April.

**[Centennial](/locations/colorado/centennial):** Cherry Creek School District graduation parties — Cherry Creek High School, Grandview, Eaglecrest, Smoky Hill — on Arapahoe County residential lots. Centennial graduation Saturdays book on the same mid-April deadline as Highlands Ranch.

**[Parker](/locations/colorado/parker):** Chaparral, Legend, and Ponderosa High School graduation parties. Parker's large residential lots and outdoor entertaining culture make it particularly well-suited for the graduation party format. Book 4–6 weeks ahead.

**[Castle Rock](/locations/colorado/castle-rock):** Douglas County's fastest-growing graduation market. Terrain, Castle Oaks, and Castle Pines communities generate increasing demand each spring. Book on the standard 4–6 week window.

**University graduation parties (Front Range):**
- CU Boulder commencement: typically third Saturday of May
- CSU Fort Collins commencement: typically fourth Saturday of May
- Book **5–7 weeks ahead** — university graduation weekends generate multi-day demand in markets with limited supply

**[Boulder](/locations/colorado/boulder):** CU Boulder commencement is the single most competitive private hibachi booking window in Boulder County. South Boulder Road estate celebrations, Chautauqua-area properties, and vacation rental events all compete for the same Saturday. Book in early April for a late May commencement.

**[Fort Collins](/locations/colorado/fort-collins):** CSU commencement generates the same demand pressure in Larimer County. Old Town rental events and Horsetooth-adjacent home celebrations compete for the same late May Saturdays. Book on the same April deadline.

## How Many Guests, and Does Group Size Matter?

Yes — group size determines whether you need one chef station or two.

**One chef station:** 8–25 guests
- Standard for most suburban graduation parties (family of 20–25 is the most common)
- Chef cooks, performs, and serves the full group sequentially

**One chef, larger groups:** 25–35 guests
- The chef can serve larger groups with adjusted service timing — note your count at booking
- All guests eat, but the performance runs slightly longer per person

**Two chef stations (strongly recommended):** 35+ guests
- Dual stations mean everyone eats at the same time
- The graduation party format in Highlands Ranch and Centennial often reaches 35–50 guests when extended family is included
- Note your anticipated count at booking — dual station configurations are available

**Practical reality:** Most graduation party hosts underestimate their count by 20–30%. Grandparents come. Neighbors join. The graduate's whole friend group stays. Book conservatively, update us as your count solidifies.

## What You Need at Your Backyard

Approximately 10×10 feet of flat outdoor clearance. Most Douglas, Arapahoe, and Jefferson County residential lots provide this easily — the chef assesses the setup area when they arrive.

**What the chef brings:**
- Propane teppan grill — fully self-contained, no gas hookup required
- All proteins: chicken, steak, shrimp, salmon, vegetables, fried rice, lo mein, miso soup, salad
- House-made ginger sauce, yum yum sauce, teriyaki
- All plates, chopsticks, and utensils

**What you provide:**
- Seating for your guests (backyard chairs, folding tables, patio furniture)
- Vehicle access to the property

## The Performance

The chef arrives 20–30 minutes before your start time to set up. The performance runs 90–120 minutes from first flame to final plate — fire tricks, the volcano, flying shrimp, precision knife work, every dish cooked to order in front of your guests.

The performance format works equally for every age in the graduation party mix: the 6-year-old who will talk about the fire volcano for the next month, the 70-year-old grandparent who has never seen teppanyaki, and the graduate's friends who pull out their phones during the shrimp flip and put it in the group chat.

Full cleanup when dinner ends.
