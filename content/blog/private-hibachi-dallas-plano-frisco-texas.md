The Dallas-Fort Worth metro has some of the best private hibachi territory in the country. The northern suburbs — [Plano](/locations/texas/plano), [Frisco](/locations/texas/frisco), [McKinney](/locations/texas/mckinney), [Allen](/locations/texas/allen), [Southlake](/locations/texas/southlake), [Colleyville](/locations/texas/colleyville) — are vast swaths of newer construction with generous outdoor spaces, three-car garages, and backyards purpose-built for the kind of outdoor entertaining that Texas climate and culture demand. When you have a 2,500 square foot outdoor living area and 40 people on the guest list, a private chef who comes to you isn't a luxury — it's the obvious call.

## The North Dallas Corridor

**[Plano](/locations/texas/plano)** is the most active private hibachi market in North Texas. The combination of corporate headquarters (Toyota, JPMorganChase, Liberty Mutual), a large South Asian and East Asian community with strong celebration culture, and suburban lot sizes that support 30–60 guest events makes Plano one of the strongest graduation and family event markets in the state.

West Plano and Legacy Corridor properties have excellent setups. East Plano older neighborhoods have smaller lots — confirm square footage.

**[Frisco](/locations/texas/frisco)** has grown from a small suburb to one of the fastest-developing cities in America. The newer developments in West Frisco (around Phillips Creek Ranch and Stonebriar) have some of the largest rear yard footprints in the DFW metro — dual hibachi stations for 40 guests are frequently workable here.

**[McKinney](/locations/texas/mckinney)** runs similar to Frisco with strong newer construction in the southwestern neighborhoods and older historic-district properties near downtown that have more variable outdoor space.

**[Allen](/locations/texas/allen)** sits between Plano and McKinney with consistent suburban lots and a strong graduation event market. Allen ISD is one of the largest school districts in the state — graduation season in May is intense.

## Park Cities and Dallas Proper

**[Highland Park](/locations/texas/highland-park) and [University Park](/locations/texas/university-park)** are where Dallas's most established wealth concentrates. The properties here — large estate homes with mature landscaping, formal outdoor entertaining terraces, and in many cases outdoor kitchens already in place — are ideal for private chef events. Guest counts at Highland Park events tend to be more intimate (12–20 guests) with high expectations for quality and presentation.

Filet and lobster upgrades are standard at this price point. The host usually wants something that matches the property.

**[Dallas](/locations/texas/dallas) proper** runs a wide range from the dense urban neighborhoods of Uptown and Oak Lawn (small yards, need confirmation) to the established homes of Preston Hollow and North Dallas (estate-scale outdoor spaces, ideal for hibachi). Confirm outdoor square footage for any Dallas city-core booking.

## Fort Worth and Western Suburbs

**[Fort Worth](/locations/texas/fort-worth)**, [Southlake](/locations/texas/southlake), [Westlake](/locations/texas/westlake), and [Colleyville](/locations/texas/colleyville) anchor the western DFW market.

Southlake and Westlake are among the wealthiest zip codes in the country. The properties here — often custom-built on 1+ acre lots — provide spectacular private event settings. These are the DFW equivalent of Paradise Valley, Arizona: estate dining where the property is part of the experience.

Fort Worth proper has a mix of historic ranch-style properties near the Cultural District with generous outdoor acreage and denser Southside/Near Southside neighborhoods that need space confirmation.

**[Arlington](/locations/texas/arlington)** sits between Dallas and Fort Worth with a mix of older suburban development and newer construction. Consistent demand around UT Arlington graduation events and family celebrations.

## Pricing in Texas

Includes the chef, full live teppanyaki performance, two proteins per person, salad, fried rice, grilled vegetables, sauces, plates, utensils, setup, and complete cleanup.

**Available upgrades:**
- Filet mignon — +$10/person
- Lobster tail — +$15/person
- Extra protein — $15/order
- Yakisoba noodles — $5/order

## Texas Outdoor Season

North Texas outdoor season runs March through November, with spring (March–May) and fall (September–November) being ideal. Texas summers (June–August) are hot but workable in the evening — plan chef arrival for 7:00–7:30 PM to catch cooler hours. December through February can work for covered patio events.
