The Research Triangle has a distinctive profile as a private dining market: a highly educated, tech-industry-heavy population with transplants from every part of the country, strong backyard entertaining culture, and a university calendar that generates consistent graduation demand from UNC, Duke, and NC State. [Raleigh](/locations/north-carolina/raleigh), [Durham](/locations/north-carolina/durham), [Cary](/locations/north-carolina/cary), [Apex](/locations/north-carolina/apex), and [Chapel Hill](/locations/north-carolina/chapel-hill) all sit within a tight geographic area and represent one of North Carolina's most active private event markets.

## Who Books in the Triangle

**Graduation dinners.** The Triangle's university calendar drives one of the most concentrated graduation dinner markets in the Southeast. UNC Chapel Hill, Duke, and NC State all hold ceremonies in May. A family hosting a graduation dinner for 20–30 guests at their Cary or North Raleigh home — grandparents from out of state, college friends, local family — is a format that runs every weekend in May.

[Cary](/locations/north-carolina/cary) and [Apex](/locations/north-carolina/apex) have the most consistently bookable graduation setups: newer suburban homes with generous rear yards and covered patio sections.

**Tech company team events.** The Triangle's Research Triangle Park and surrounding corridor houses major employers including SAS, Lenovo, Cisco, and Red Hat. Corporate team dinners at an employee's home — Raleigh or Cary — are a recurring format for year-end celebrating and team building.

**Birthday parties.** The 30s-through-50s professional demographic in the Triangle produces strong birthday party demand. A hibachi chef at a Raleigh backyard for a 40th birthday — 15–25 guests, great food, something nobody's done before — is an increasingly standard format.

**[Durham](/locations/north-carolina/durham) event culture.** Durham's food culture is unusually strong for a mid-sized city, which means Durham residents have high expectations for a dinner event. Private hibachi meets those expectations: the food quality is genuine, the live performance is engaging, and the whole experience has a distinctiveness that Durham diners appreciate.

## Setup Across the Triangle

**Cary and Apex** have the Triangle's best backyard profiles for hibachi — newer construction (1990s–2010s), standard suburban lots with 15×25+ foot rear yards, patio surfaces adjacent to the house. Single-station hibachi for 10–20 guests is straightforward in these neighborhoods.

**North Raleigh** (North Hills, Falls, Stonehenge) runs similar to Cary — good lot sizes, established neighborhoods with patio setups. Inner North Raleigh and the Five Points area skew toward older homes with more compact yards; confirm space in advance.

**Durham** has the most varied setup profile. The older neighborhoods near Duke — Trinity Park, Forest Hills, Hope Valley — tend toward mature landscaped yards that work well. The denser urban areas of downtown Durham and East Durham need advance confirmation.

**Chapel Hill and [Holly Springs](/locations/north-carolina/holly-springs)** have established residential lots with generally adequate outdoor space. Chapel Hill's older neighborhoods near UNC have the same vintage yard variability as Durham.
