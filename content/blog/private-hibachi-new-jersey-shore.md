The Jersey Shore beach house week has one consistent problem: feeding a group of 12–20 people who don't want to fight for a restaurant table on a Saturday night in season. The best restaurants along the shore are booked weeks out. The others have an hour-and-a-half wait. And after a day at the beach, everyone wants to stay at the house.

Private hibachi solves this. A certified teppanyaki chef arrives at your rental, sets up on the deck or patio, and runs a full live cooking show and dinner for your group while you stay exactly where you are. It's the best dinner of the week — and you don't have to leave.

## Shore Communities We Serve

**[Stone Harbor](/locations/new-jersey/stone-harbor) and [Avalon](/locations/new-jersey/avalon)** are the premier end of the Jersey Shore market. The properties here skew toward larger weekly rentals and second homes — elevated rear decks, ground-floor patios, and outdoor spaces designed for serious entertaining. Groups in Stone Harbor and Avalon tend toward 10–20 guests with high expectations for the food. Filet and lobster upgrades are common here.

**[Cape May](/locations/new-jersey/cape-may)** is architecturally distinct — Victorian bed-and-breakfast territory with a vacation rental stock that includes converted historic homes with private rear yards and garden spaces. Many Cape May vacation homes have enclosed garden courtyards; confirm overhead clearance when booking (teppanyaki requires open-sky ventilation).

**[Ocean City](/locations/new-jersey/ocean-city)** is a large family-focused resort town with an enormous rental inventory. Ocean City groups tend to run larger — 15–25 guests — and the properties are generally direct-access beach homes with open rear decks or ground-level patios. This is some of the most straightforward setup territory on the shore.

**[Sea Isle City](/locations/new-jersey/sea-isle-city) and [Wildwood](/locations/new-jersey/wildwood)** run a younger, louder demographic than Avalon or Cape May. Bachelor parties, bachelorette weekends, and friend group week rentals are common formats. Private hibachi gives these groups a dinner event that gathers everyone before the evening continues.

**[Asbury Park](/locations/new-jersey/asbury-park), [Point Pleasant](/locations/new-jersey/point-pleasant), and [Spring Lake](/locations/new-jersey/spring-lake)** anchor the northern end of the shore. Asbury Park has the most varied rental stock — Victorian homes with rear yards, mid-century bungalows, newer construction near the boardwalk. Spring Lake skews more historic and estate-style, similar to Cape May in character.

**[Long Branch](/locations/new-jersey/long-branch) and [Belmar](/locations/new-jersey/belmar)** sit between Asbury and Point Pleasant. Active rental markets with consistent summer demand.

## What Works on a Shore Deck

Most Jersey Shore rental properties have one or more of the following outdoor spaces:

- **Rear ground-level deck:** Most common and most flexible for hibachi setup. As long as it's flat and has at least 10×10 feet of clearance, it works.
- **Elevated deck (second or third floor):** Works well if the deck is fully open-air with no overhead obstruction. Confirm that the deck has open sky above — not a screened porch ceiling.
- **Ground-level patio:** Any flat hard surface adjacent to the house works.

**What doesn't work:** Fully enclosed screened porches with no open-air section. If your rental's only outdoor space is an enclosed screen room, confirm in advance.

## Book Early for Shore Season

Summer shore weeks — especially July and the first two weeks of August — are the highest-demand window on the entire East Coast calendar. Book your hibachi chef as soon as your rental week is confirmed. 5–6 weeks minimum for July and peak August dates. Some popular shore weekends fill further in advance.

Shoulder season (late May–June and September) has more flexibility and often better weather for outdoor events.
