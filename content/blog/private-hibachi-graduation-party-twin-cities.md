## Group Sizes and Setup

Twin Cities graduation parties typically run 25–55 guests. Here's how the format scales:

**25 guests or fewer:** One chef. The performance runs 90 minutes. Everyone eats within the same 30-minute window from first plate to last. Full fire performance for the full crowd.

**25–40 guests:** One chef handles this range comfortably. Note your count at booking — the chef will plan service timing accordingly.

**40–60 guests:** Dual chef stations are strongly recommended. Two chefs running simultaneously means everyone eats at the same time rather than in two waves. Dual-station events run the same 90–120 minute performance window.

**60+ guests:** Dual stations required. Book early — large-format graduation events have the most limited availability windows.

**Backyard requirements:** The teppan setup needs approximately 10×10 feet of flat outdoor clearance. A typical Twin Cities backyard lawn, patio, or driveway all work. No gas hookup required — the chef uses a self-contained propane grill.

## How the Evening Works

Here's the typical graduation party timeline:

**3:00–4:00 PM:** Chef arrives. Setup takes 20–30 minutes. The chef doesn't need assistance — they set up the teppan station, organize proteins, and prepare everything independently while your guests arrive and settle in.

**4:00–4:30 PM:** Guests gather around the teppan. The chef begins the performance.

**4:30–6:00 PM:** Full performance — 90 minutes of live cooking. Fire tricks, flying shrimp, precision knife work. Every protein cooked to order in front of your guests. Everyone eats during the performance window.

**6:00–6:30 PM:** Chef cleans the cooking area completely and removes all equipment. Your backyard is exactly as it was. The evening continues.

The hibachi performance doesn't require your guests to do anything — no standing in lines, no buffet management, no plates running out. The chef serves every guest during the performance window.
